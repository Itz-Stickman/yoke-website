Yoke for Roku · Beta

Privacy Policy

Effective November 21, 2026

The short version
  • No account, no sign-up, no ads.
  • No analytics, tracking or advertising IDs. We, the people who make Yoke, receive nothing from the TV app.
  • Your TV talks to Yoke on your own PC or phone, over your home network. Your music, playlists and listening history stay on those devices.

This policy covers the Yoke channel for Roku TVs and Roku streaming players (“Yoke for Roku”, “the app”). Yoke for Roku is a remote screen for the Yoke music player: it plays the music you keep in Yoke on your Windows PC or Android phone (“your Yoke device”). It does not have music of its own.

What we collect

Nothing. Yoke for Roku has no servers of ours to send anything to. It doesn't collect analytics, crash reports, usage statistics, advertising identifiers, your location or your Roku account details, and it shows no ads.

What's kept on your TV

To remember its settings, the app saves a few small things in your TV's storage for this app:

  • The network address and name of the Yoke device it's paired with (for example “192.168.1.20” and “Living room PC”).
  • A pairing key, which lets your Yoke device know the requests come from your paired TV.
  • Your choices: accent colour, the last visualizer you used, and any names you put on the Yule Log visualizer's stockings.

These never leave your TV, except the pairing key, which goes only to your Yoke device with each request.

What your TV shares with your Yoke device

When you pair, your TV sends your Yoke device its name as set in Roku settings (for example “Living room TV”), so you can tell it apart from other devices. After that, the app asks your Yoke device for your library, covers, lyrics, playlists, podcasts, radio favourites and the songs themselves, and tells it what's playing and where you are in the song, so Yoke on your PC or phone can show it and act as a remote. Podcast listening progress is saved on your Yoke device. All of this goes across your home network only, directly between your TV and your Yoke device.

Your home network carries this traffic without encryption, as is usual for devices on the same Wi-Fi. Use Yoke on a network you trust. A pairing code shown on your TV and a pairing key keep other devices from using your Yoke device's TV connection.

Services outside your home

Some features need information from the internet. Your Yoke device fetches it, not your TV, and only what each feature needs is sent:

  • Lyrics: the song's title, artist, album and length go to LRCLIB.
  • Cover art and artist pictures (when a song has none): artist, album and song names go to the Deezer and Apple iTunes search services.
  • Internet radio: station searches go to the Radio Browser directory, and the station's stream comes from the station.
  • Podcasts: show feeds come from the podcast's publisher.

One exception: podcast episodes play straight from the podcast publisher's server to your TV, so that server sees your home's internet (IP) address, as it would with any podcast app. Each of these services has its own privacy policy.

Roku

Your Roku device and Roku's services collect information under Roku's Privacy Policy, separately from Yoke. Yoke doesn't receive any of it.

Children

Yoke for Roku isn't directed at children under 13, and since it collects no personal information, it collects none from children either.

Removing your information

  • To unpair, choose Settings → Disconnect this TV in the app. This deletes the saved address and pairing key from your TV.
  • Removing the Yoke channel from your Roku deletes everything the app saved on your TV.
  • Your library, playlists and listening history live in Yoke on your PC or phone, where you can change or delete them.

Beta

Yoke for Roku is in beta. If a later version handles information differently, we'll update this page and the date above before that version comes out. If a change means the app would collect information about you, we'll say so plainly here first.
